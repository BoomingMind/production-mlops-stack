# Common Air Pollutants and Their Health Effects

## Particulate Matter (PM)

### PM2.5 (Fine Particulate Matter)
Particles smaller than 2.5 micrometers in diameter.

**Sources:**
- Vehicle exhaust and diesel engines
- Power plants burning fossil fuels
- Industrial processes
- Wildfires and wood burning
- Cooking and heating
- Dust from construction and agriculture

**Health Effects:**
- Penetrates deep into lungs and bloodstream
- Aggravates asthma and causes respiratory symptoms
- Contributes to cardiovascular disease
- May cause irregular heartbeat
- Associated with premature death
- Long-term exposure linked to lung cancer

**Who is at risk:**
- Children (developing lungs)
- Elderly (reduced lung function)
- People with heart disease
- People with lung disease (asthma, COPD)
- Pregnant women (effects on fetus)

### PM10 (Coarse Particulate Matter)
Particles between 2.5 and 10 micrometers in diameter.

**Sources:**
- Dust from roads and construction
- Agricultural activities
- Mining operations
- Pollen and mold spores

**Health Effects:**
- Irritates airways
- Triggers asthma attacks
- Causes coughing and difficulty breathing
- Less dangerous than PM2.5 but still harmful

## Ground-Level Ozone (O3)

Formed when pollutants from vehicles, power plants, and industrial sources react with sunlight.

**Peak Times:**
- Warm, sunny afternoons
- Summer months
- Urban areas with heavy traffic

**Health Effects:**
- Reduces lung function
- Triggers asthma attacks
- Causes chest pain, coughing, throat irritation
- Makes lungs more susceptible to infection
- Aggravates chronic lung diseases
- May cause permanent lung damage with long-term exposure

**Protection Tips:**
- Limit outdoor activities during peak ozone hours (afternoon)
- Exercise in the morning or evening
- Avoid areas near heavy traffic

## Carbon Monoxide (CO)

Colorless, odorless gas from incomplete combustion.

**Sources:**
- Vehicle exhaust
- Fuel-burning appliances
- Tobacco smoke
- Fires and wildfires

**Health Effects:**
- Reduces oxygen delivery to organs
- Causes headaches and dizziness
- Impairs vision and concentration
- At high levels: confusion, unconsciousness, death
- Especially dangerous for people with heart disease

## Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2)

Reddish-brown gas with sharp odor.

**Sources:**
- Vehicle emissions
- Power plants
- Industrial facilities
- Kerosene heaters
- Gas stoves

**Health Effects:**
- Irritates airways
- Aggravates respiratory conditions
- Contributes to asthma development in children
- Increases susceptibility to respiratory infections
- Forms ground-level ozone and particulate matter

## Sulfur Dioxide (SO2)

Colorless gas with sharp smell.

**Sources:**
- Burning fossil fuels (coal, oil)
- Industrial processes
- Volcanoes

**Health Effects:**
- Irritates nose, throat, airways
- Causes coughing and wheezing
- Aggravates asthma
- Contributes to acid rain
- Forms particulate matter in atmosphere

## Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)

Gases emitted from various solids and liquids.

**Sources:**
- Paints and solvents
- Cleaning products
- Pesticides
- Building materials
- Gasoline and vehicle emissions
- Personal care products

**Health Effects:**
- Eye, nose, throat irritation
- Headaches and nausea
- Liver and kidney damage (long-term)
- Some VOCs are carcinogenic
- Contributes to ozone formation

## Indoor Air Pollutants

Indoor air can be 2-5 times more polluted than outdoor air.

**Common Indoor Pollutants:**
- Radon (from soil)
- Environmental tobacco smoke
- Mold and mildew
- Pet dander
- Dust mites
- Cleaning products
- Carbon monoxide (faulty appliances)

**Improving Indoor Air Quality:**
- Increase ventilation (when outdoor air is clean)
- Use air purifiers with HEPA filters
- Control humidity (30-50%)
- Avoid smoking indoors
- Regular cleaning and dusting
- Proper maintenance of HVAC systems
- Use low-VOC products
