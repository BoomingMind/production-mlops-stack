# Health Precautions Based on AQI Levels

## General Recommendations for All AQI Levels

Regardless of AQI levels, these practices help protect your respiratory health:
- Stay informed about daily air quality forecasts
- Keep indoor air clean using air purifiers with HEPA filters
- Keep windows and doors closed when outdoor air quality is poor
- Avoid using candles, fireplaces, or other sources of indoor combustion
- Maintain your HVAC system and replace filters regularly

## Precautions by AQI Category

### Good AQI (0-50)
- No restrictions on outdoor activities
- Great time for outdoor exercise and recreation
- Open windows to ventilate your home if desired

### Moderate AQI (51-100)
- Unusually sensitive individuals should consider reducing prolonged outdoor exertion
- People with asthma should have rescue inhalers readily available
- Consider indoor exercise if you experience symptoms

### Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (101-150)
Sensitive groups include:
- Children and teenagers
- Adults 65 years and older
- People with lung disease (asthma, COPD)
- People with heart disease
- Pregnant women
- Outdoor workers

Precautions for sensitive groups:
- Reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion
- Take more breaks during outdoor activities
- Watch for symptoms like coughing, shortness of breath
- Keep quick-relief medicine handy

General public:
- Consider reducing intense outdoor exercise
- Opt for indoor alternatives when possible

### Unhealthy AQI (151-200)
For everyone:
- Reduce prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors
- Take more breaks during outdoor activities
- Schedule outdoor activities for times when air quality is better

For sensitive groups:
- Avoid prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors
- Move activities indoors or reschedule
- Consider wearing N95 masks if outdoor exposure is unavoidable

### Very Unhealthy AQI (201-300)
For everyone:
- Avoid prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion
- Move physical activities indoors
- Reduce exercise intensity

For sensitive groups:
- Avoid all outdoor physical activity
- Stay indoors with windows and doors closed
- Run air purifiers if available
- Seek medical attention if experiencing symptoms

### Hazardous AQI (301+)
For everyone:
- Avoid all outdoor physical activity
- Stay indoors with doors and windows closed
- Use air purifiers with HEPA filters
- Create a clean room if possible
- Wear N95 masks if outdoor exposure is absolutely necessary

Emergency precautions:
- Have emergency supplies ready (medication, water, food)
- Monitor news and emergency alerts
- Be prepared to evacuate if conditions worsen
- Seek medical attention immediately for severe symptoms

## Symptoms to Watch For

During poor air quality, monitor for these symptoms:
- Coughing or throat irritation
- Shortness of breath or difficulty breathing
- Chest tightness or pain
- Wheezing
- Unusual fatigue
- Headache
- Eye, nose, or throat irritation
- Dizziness

If symptoms persist or worsen, seek medical attention.

## Special Considerations

### For Children
- Children breathe more rapidly and are more active outdoors
- Their lungs are still developing, making them more vulnerable
- Limit outdoor playtime during high AQI days
- Ensure schools have air quality policies

### For Elderly
- Age-related decline in lung function increases vulnerability
- Pre-existing conditions may be aggravated
- Limit outdoor activities and stay cool indoors
- Have medications readily available

### For Outdoor Workers
- Monitor local AQI regularly throughout the day
- Take frequent breaks in clean air environments
- Wear appropriate respiratory protection (N95 or better)
- Increase water intake
- Report symptoms to supervisors

### For Athletes and Active Individuals
- Check AQI before outdoor workouts
- Exercise early morning when ozone levels are typically lower
- Reduce workout intensity on poor air quality days
- Consider indoor alternatives (gym, indoor pool)
- Avoid exercising near high-traffic areas
